Fall started with the first official conference badge powered by AtomVM, handed out to
attendees at [Goatmire 2026](https://2026.goatmire.com/). We have also released
[AtomVM v0.7.0-beta.0](https://github.com/atomvm/AtomVM/releases/tag/v0.7.0-beta.0) and made
several quality-of-life improvements to [exatomvm](https://github.com/atomvm/exatomvm), our Mix
plugin for Elixir projects.

<!--more-->

## The Goatmire badge

We finally have an official AtomVM conference badge! [Gus Workman](https://github.com/gworkman) at
[Protolux Electronics](https://protolux.io/) designed it with AtomVM in mind from the start.
Built around an ESP32-S3, it has a 320x240 color display and a QWERTY keyboard, plus four RGB
LEDs and infrared communication. The badge also has a temperature sensor and an accelerometer,
with a Qwiic connector for adding more sensors.

The firmware is written in Elixir and includes contact sharing, chat and the conference
schedule. There are Windows 95 and classic Macintosh themes to choose from, plus an
application for forming Erlang clusters between badges. Firmware updates can be delivered
over the air through [NervesHub](https://nerves-hub.org/).

Elixir and the AtomVM tools made it easy to extend the firmware, and attendees wasted no time
making it their own. People were adding games during the conference, including Connect Four
and a racing game, then walking around with their own versions of the firmware. We were very happy
to see people having this much fun with AtomVM!

You don't need a badge on your desk to try the firmware, either. The
[badge repository](https://github.com/protolux-electronics/avm_badge) includes a browser-based
simulator. You can edit the Elixir code and try your changes without flashing a board each time.

AtomVM was also the subject of the four-hour
[AtomVM Is Real](https://2026.goatmire.com/talk/atomvm-is-real) workshop and the talk
[AtomVM: When Constrained Doesn't Mean Boring](https://2026.goatmire.com/talk/atomvm-when-constrained-doesnt-mean-boring),
both presented by [Davide Bettio](https://uninstall.it/). Missed the workshop? The
[slides and exercises](https://uninstall.it/goatmire-2026-workshop.html) are online, so you can
work through them at home, from your first application to reading sensors and publishing
data over MQTT.

## Making exatomvm easier to use

The conference and workshop were also a good opportunity to introduce several quality-of-life
improvements in [exatomvm](https://github.com/atomvm/exatomvm/). We tackled some of the annoying
parts of getting started, such as finding the right serial port, looking up flash offsets and
setting up a separate serial terminal.

Start with `mix atomvm`: it now explains the workflow and points you to the tasks you need.
For ESP32 boards, `mix atomvm.esp32.install` installs prebuilt firmware without an ESP-IDF
setup. You can choose a stable release, a prerelease or a nightly build, including images with
AtomGL. Add `--version v0.7.0-beta.0` to select the beta instead of the default stable release.

Both install and flash can find the connected USB device automatically. And
`mix atomvm.esp32.flash` reads the partition table to find where your application belongs.
You no longer need to look up the flash offset yourself.

After flashing, `mix atomvm.esp32.monitor` shows console output without a separate serial
terminal. When a task needs an optional dependency such as `pythonx` or `req`, exatomvm tells you
what to add.

To summarize, once your project is configured, you can install AtomVM on your ESP32, build and
flash your application and open the serial monitor with just four commands:

```sh
mix deps.get
mix atomvm.esp32.install --version v0.7.0-beta.0
mix atomvm.esp32.flash
mix atomvm.esp32.monitor
```

The flash task compiles and packs your application automatically. After changing your code,
just run the flash task again. There is no need to reinstall AtomVM.

A note before installing: installation erases flash by default. On compatible installations,
`--update` keeps your application, NVS data and partition layout. 

We also fixed a confusing source of compatibility warnings. exatomvm used to keep its own
list of supported functions and BEAM instructions, which could fall behind the AtomVM version
you were using. Now your project can declare its target release in `mix.exs`:

```elixir
{:atomvm, "~> 0.7.0-beta.0", runtime: false}
```

The [AtomVM package](https://hex.pm/packages/atomvm) supplies that release's compatibility
metadata to `mix atomvm.check`. The check also runs before packing or flashing, so its warnings
follow the release your project targets.

## AtomVM v0.7.0-beta.0

AtomVM [v0.7.0-beta.0](https://github.com/atomvm/AtomVM/releases/tag/v0.7.0-beta.0),
the first beta release in the v0.7.0 series, is now available.

This release adds a JIT backend for Xtensa on ESP32 and a WebAssembly JIT backend for the
Emscripten platform. Erlang distribution can now use UART, and USB CDC port drivers are
available for ESP32, RP2 and STM32.

Other additions include process aliases and map comprehensions. ESP32 applications can scan
for nearby Wi-Fi networks with `network:wifi_scan/0,1`, and the HTTP client now supports
chunked responses. The release also includes fixes for supervisor restarts and HTTP response
handling. See the [full changelog](https://github.com/atomvm/AtomVM/blob/v0.7.0-beta.0/CHANGELOG.md)
for the complete list of changes.

Prebuilt binaries are available on the
[GitHub release page](https://github.com/atomvm/AtomVM/releases/tag/v0.7.0-beta.0).
This is still a prerelease. Please [report any issues](https://github.com/atomvm/AtomVM/issues)
you find while testing. When upgrading from v0.6.x, read
[UPDATING.md](https://github.com/atomvm/AtomVM/blob/v0.7.0-beta.0/UPDATING.md) first.

Thank you to Gus and the badge team, everyone who joined us at Goatmire and all our contributors.
